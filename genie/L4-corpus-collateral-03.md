# Pool enclosure, the most visible install

**Topic.** How a pool enclosure install differs from roofline, and the facts that decide the job
**Audience.** Certified contractor (L4)
**Voice.** Field overview, published M03 L3.1.

**The headline.** A pool enclosure turns the whole cage into a color canvas, and it is the most visible install you will do. The track rides the enclosure's own beams, overhead and often over water, and every inch of it is seen from directly below.

**How it differs from roofline.**

| Factor | Pool Enclosure | Roofline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mounting | Track on the enclosure beams, measured beam by beam | Track on fascia |
| Viewing angle | Seen from directly below, so alignment shows more than anywhere else | Seen from the driveway, horizontal plane |
| Control | **One-Port Controller.** Power and signal meet at the T-connector | Four-Port Controller |
| Water | Every track connection gets **dielectric grease**; the track fills and drains slowly | No standing water concern |

**Getting up there safely.**
- **Walk board.** Most enclosures need one. Rated for you, your tools, and the track; both ends supported and secured.
- **Ladder on the board.** Only when it fits both feet, is secured, and is footed by a second person.
- **Never.** Stand on the screen, the gutter, or the thin horizontal members.
- **Commercial pools.** Close the area with management and keep guests out.

**The key specs (addressing vs power).**
- **Addressing per controller.** 1,000 ft. One One-Port Controller can address up to 1,000 ft of track.
- **Power per power supply.** ~300 ft at 170 W usable (85% of a 200 W PS).
- **Pro rule.** Addressing and power are different numbers. One controller addresses 1,000 ft; power still decides the power supply count. Count both separately at the take-off.

**Partial KBG-16 resolution.** The 1,000 ft addressing-per-controller figure is now PUBLISHED. It is one of the twelve numbers BRI-367 tracks and applies to any One-Port system (pool enclosure, soffit, deck). Four-Port controller addressing per port not stated in this PDF; still open.

# Tight and secure is the goal, not torque

**Topic.** How to measure, cut, and mount pool enclosure track into aluminum beams
**Audience.** Certified contractor (L4)
**Voice.** Field card, published M03 L3.2.

**The headline.** Tight and secure is the goal, NOT torque. The track screws into the side of the U-channel, tucked in the shadow of the uprights and purlins so the fasteners disappear from below.

**On every beam.**
1. **Measure.** Each beam on its own. Enclosures are NOT as square as they look.
2. **Cut.** Pool enclosure track is too thick for snips. Miter saw with an aluminum blade, piece clamped, on the ground.
3. **Place.** Screw into the SIDE of the U-channel, in the shadow of the uprights and purlins.
4. **Fasten.** Snug against the aluminum, no distortion around the screw head, then STOP.
5. **Check.** Spacing and alignment from directly below.

**Do's and don'ts.**
- ✓ Stop at snug.
- ✓ Hide fasteners in the shadow of the framing.
- ✓ Check alignment from below.
- ✗ Keep turning after snug.
- ✗ Cut on the walk board.
- ✗ Assume every beam is the same length.

**Common mistake.** Overtightening into aluminum. Once it strips, the screw stops holding no matter how hard you drive it.

# How a pool enclosure system goes together

**Topic.** The One-Port layout for pool enclosure (shared with Soffit and Deck)
**Audience.** Certified contractor (L4)
**Voice.** Assembly guide, published M03 L3.3.

**The One-Port layout.** This is the layout for pool enclosure, soffit, and deck — three systems share the same assembly pattern.

**Headline specs.**
- 170 W usable per 200 W power supply (85% rule holds).
- ~300 ft of track per power supply.
- 5 ft or less between amplifiers.

**Power feed into the T-connector.**
200 W power supply → 2-pin to 3-pin adapter → T-connector.

**Signal feed into the T-connector.**
One-Port Controller → 3-pin to 3-pin adapter → T-connector.

**The run.**
T-connector → Signal amplifier → Lead line, up to 5 ft → Signal amplifier → Lead line, if needed → Light strand on beam 1 → Signal amplifier → Lead line to the next beam.

**Pro tip.** The run may need extra lead line and amplifiers BEFORE the first beam, and every lead line between beams starts with a signal amplifier.

**How it changes on different enclosures.**

- **Small screened patio.** Short run, one power supply, often reachable from ladders alone.
- **Full cage with many beams.** Walk-board setup, beam by beam. Test each section before you move the board (see test-as-you-go checklist).
- **Two-story or mansard cage.** Taller beams may need a lift, in the quote. More transitions, more amplifiers.
- **Commercial or HOA pool.** Close the pool with management, keep guests out, follow site rules.

# Every walk-board move loses access to the section behind

**Topic.** The test discipline for enclosure installs — test before you move the board
**Audience.** Certified contractor (L4)
**Voice.** Field checklist, published M03 L3.3.

**The headline rule.** On an enclosure, every time you move the walk board you lose easy access to the section behind you. Test BEFORE you move it.

**Every beam.**
- Connections greased (dielectric) and seated.
- Beam lit, full color range.
- Alignment checked from below.

**Before the walk board moves.**
- This section tested.
- Nothing left on the board or over the water.

**End of day.**
- Whole cage lit.
- Area clear and reopened.

**Why this matters.** A failed connection discovered after you've moved the board costs you a full repositioning. A failed connection discovered BEFORE you move costs you two minutes. Test early, test often, and never cross water with a tool bag you still need overhead.

# The reveal is where the customer falls in love with the job

**Topic.** Post-install walkthrough and handoff on a pool enclosure
**Audience.** Certified contractor (L4)
**Voice.** Handoff card, published M03 L3.4.

**The headline.** The reveal is where the customer falls in love with the job. Show the enclosure from every place they will actually see it.

**Show it from three places.**
1. From the pool deck.
2. From inside the cage, looking up.
3. From inside the house, through the glass.

**Before you leave.**

- **The app.** Their scenes, whites, and schedules. Walk through it on their phone, not yours.
- **The warranty.** The written terms, per component (per L4-RULE-WARRANTY-COMPONENT-MAP). Note: track and strand are 10 years; One-Port Controller is 3 years; signal amplifiers are 10 years.
- **Maintenance.** What the program includes (per L4-VOICE-MAINTENANCE-PROGRAM, which the contractor sells separately).
- **Next phase.** Anything else they mentioned on the walk. A pool enclosure usually opens the door to roofline, soffit, or landscape on the same property.

**Why this matters.** The reveal is also the moment they tell you what else they want lit. Many L4 handoffs are the start of the next quote.
