# Winning Lighting Installer Sites Beat Dealer Pages

The best permanent lighting installer websites in the United States share a compact skeleton (Home, a dedicated permanent or roofline lighting service page, Gallery, About, FAQ, Blog, Contact or Free Quote, Privacy) and then separate themselves with three things almost no competitor does: a Google review count in the hero, real city pages with local copy and a map, and any form of price transparency or financing. Across 14 top-ranking dealer and corporate sites inventoried in Phoenix, Dallas, Orlando, Denver, Houston, Atlanta and Chicago, Gallery and About appeared on 12, FAQ on 11, Blog on 10, while a dedicated Reviews page, a Financing page, a Warranty page, a Pricing page and a live demo page each appeared on three or fewer ([Trimlight DFW](https://trimlightdfw.com/), [Get Lit](https://getlitdecorativelighting.com/), [Stay Off The Roof](https://stayofftheroof.com/)). Manufacturer dealer pages on trimlight.com, gemstonelights.com and jellyfishlighting.com are lead-capture location pages the dealer does not own, and they universally lack published pricing, a named financing partner, online booking, an instant estimate, service-area pages and dealer identity ([Trimlight Dealer Locations](https://trimlight.com/dealer-locations), [JellyFish Find a Dealer](https://jellyfishlighting.com/find-a-dealer)). A new contractor's homeowner-facing site should therefore launch with roughly 20 to 30 URLs (the core skeleton, one Pricing page with a per-foot range, a Financing page with a named lender, a Warranty page splitting product and labor coverage, 8 to 15 city pages, three to five use-case pages, and a Reviews page), plus a sticky tap-to-call and tap-to-text bar, an embedded Google review feed, a photo-upload preview or estimate tool, and a 60-second lead-response protocol. Published market figures put installed permanent lighting at **$20 to $40 per linear foot** and the average residential ticket at **$4,000 to $8,000**, with Strandr citing a **$7,500** average permanent install and Oelo citing **$5,000+** ([Thunder Lighting Supply](https://thunderlightingsupply.com/how-much-do-permanent-lights-cost/), [Strandr](https://www.strandr.com/blog/permanent-lighting-year-round-revenue-contractor-guide), [Oelo Franchise](https://www.oelo.com/outdoor-lighting-franchise-opportunities/)). Home services websites convert **7.8 percent** on average ([WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/blog/home-services/home-services-marketing-benchmarks/)), so a county-leading site that pulls a few hundred peak-season visits a month is worth double-digit quote requests a month at a $5,000 ticket, which is the basis for valuing it.

## Twelve of fourteen top sites share an eight-page skeleton

The inventory covered dealer sites for every major manufacturer (Trimlight, Gemstone, JellyFish, EverLights, Oelo, CelebRight), one national franchise (Outdoor Lighting Perspectives), one independent multi-metro Texas installer (The Perfect Light) and three corporate city landing pages. The universal set is narrow. **Gallery or Our Work appeared on 12 of 14, About on 12, FAQ on 11, Blog on 10 and Commercial on 11** ([Trimlight DFW](https://trimlightdfw.com/), [Stay Off The Roof](https://stayofftheroof.com/), [JellyFish Houston](https://www.jellyfishlighting.com/houston), [OLP Phoenix](https://www.outdoorlights.com/phoenix/residential/permanent-roofline-lighting/)). Everything past that skeleton is where sites diverge, and the divergence tracks with how well they rank. Financing, Warranty and Reviews as standalone pages each showed up on only three or four sites, Support or Troubleshooting on five, HOA documents on four, per-city service area URLs on six, competitor comparison pages on one (Trimlight DFW, with five pages against Everlights, Gemstone, Jellyfish, Oelo and Govee), and a live demo page on one ([Trimlight DFW](https://trimlightdfw.com/), [Get Lit](https://getlitdecorativelighting.com/)).

The most complete dealer site found is Get Lit Decorative Lighting in Orlando, which carries Financing, Warranty and Registration, HOA Documents, Video Tutorials, Troubleshooting, RGB Codes, Careers, Contractor Partners and a per-service gallery, and which links 24 Central Florida towns individually from a "Where We Work" block ([Get Lit](https://getlitdecorativelighting.com/)). The pattern that matters is that the strongest independent dealers replicate the manufacturer's Support, App, Warranty and HOA resource pages on their own domain so the homeowner never leaves. The thinnest sites in the sample (Light Up LLC, IAWL Permanent, Lighting of Atlanta) were single-page or seven-link sites with no reviews, no warranty and no financing on the homepage ([Light Up LLC](https://www.lightupllc.com/), [IAWL Permanent](https://iawlpermanent.com/), [Lighting of Atlanta](https://www.lightingofatlanta.com/)).

Two structural patterns govern seasonal and color content. Trimlight-affiliated sites build one templated page per holiday (13 event pages on Trimlight DFW, each about 2,000 words with a 4.9 star badge, four testimonials, three spacing tiers and six FAQs) plus a "Used For" taxonomy ([Trimlight DFW Halloween](https://trimlightdfw.com/events/halloween-lights/)). JellyFish, Gemstone and EverLights dealers fold seasons into a single Holiday or Functions page and make color tangible with a homepage "same house, many colors" carousel, which Get Lit does with Daytime, Warm White, Christmas, 4th of July, Halloween, St. Patrick's and Valentine's tabs ([Get Lit](https://getlitdecorativelighting.com/), [Level 5 Lighting](https://level5lighting.com/)). Per-holiday pages are an SEO play, one URL per "permanent Halloween lights [city]" query, not a UX requirement. Game Day is treated as its own use case at the same level as Holiday and Security on JellyFish, Trimlight, Level 5 and Light Up sites, which matters in a college football county.

Every strong site presents the install service the same way. A "How It Works" or "3-Step Process" block (Consultation, Measure and Manufacture, Installation) appears on Trimlight DFW, Stay Off The Roof, Outdoor Ambiance, EverLights, OLP and Lighting of Atlanta, and spacing tiers (6, 9 and 12 inch on Trimlight, 6, 9 and 16 inch on JellyFish dealers) serve as a pricing ladder in place of published prices ([Trimlight DFW](https://trimlightdfw.com/), [Level 5 Lighting](https://level5lighting.com/)). Dealer sites lead with the crew and the city ("Phoenix's Outdoor Lighting Installers, We design, you shine") and use the manufacturer as a badge, while corporate location pages lead with specs. The best dealers explicitly split manufacturer product warranty from their own labor warranty and put their install count, years and license number next to the badge, as Stay Off The Roof does with "1,398 completed installations," "25+ years," ROC #344820 and a free first-year maintenance visit ([Stay Off The Roof](https://stayofftheroof.com/)).

## Review count in the hero separates page-one sites from thin ones

The single most consistent proof element on the higher-ranking sites is a Google star rating with a review count in the hero. **Trimlight DFW shows 4.9 stars and 358 reviews, Stay Off The Roof 4.9 and 291, JellyFish Houston 4.7 and 1,300+, OLP Phoenix 4,489 reviews**, each with links to Google, Facebook or Yelp ([Trimlight DFW](https://trimlightdfw.com/), [Stay Off The Roof](https://stayofftheroof.com/), [JellyFish Houston](https://www.jellyfishlighting.com/houston), [OLP Phoenix](https://www.outdoorlights.com/phoenix/residential/permanent-roofline-lighting/)). The sites without it were the thinnest in the sample. Behind the rating sits a stat row (installs, years, warranty), a named-testimonial carousel, a photo gallery, a warranty badge and, in Arizona, a state license number in the header ([Level 5 Lighting](https://level5lighting.com/)). Stay Off The Roof goes one step further with a public dealer scorecard, "98% satisfaction rate across 117 verified surveys" and Professionalism 98.6 percent, which mirrors the survey-verified score Gemstone publishes on its dealer pages ([Stay Off The Roof](https://stayofftheroof.com/), [Gemstone dealer page](https://www.gemstonelights.com/dealers/brilliant-solutions/)).

The bar for review volume is lower than in other trades. One category guide reports that **50 to 100 Google reviews places an installer ahead of 90 percent of competitors** and that most Christmas light markets need only about 50 reviews to rank first, versus 150+ for pressure washing, and that NFC tap cards convert 60 to 70 percent of asks versus 10 to 20 percent for email ([ChristmasLights.io 2026](https://christmaslights.io/post/marketing-in-2026-how-to-grow-your-christmas-lights-business)). Reviews should be asked for twice, right after install and again after the first big holiday when the customer wants to show the house off, and coached to mention the city and product words like soffit, app and RGB so they feed the city page ([Piggybank SEO](https://piggybankseo.com/permanent-outdoor-lighting-seo-get-more-installs-from-google-and-maps/)). Consumer data supports the emphasis, with **84 percent using Google to find reviews, 63 percent expecting a response within a week, and 75 percent or more consuming video** when researching a local business ([BrightLocal 2025](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/)). A Housecall Pro survey of 1,040 homeowners found **92 percent are influenced by visuals of past work and 68 percent expect photo or video proof**, and 96 percent expect a professional website ([Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-customer-service-report-trends-statistics/)).

True before-and-after pairs are rare in the category. Only Trimlight DFW ("What does Trimlight look like during the day?"), Get Lit (a Daytime tab) and Level 5 ("Discreet from Street") explicitly address daytime appearance on the homepage, and the industry substitute for before/after is the same house shown in two colors ([Trimlight DFW](https://trimlightdfw.com/), [Get Lit](https://getlitdecorativelighting.com/), [Level 5 Lighting](https://level5lighting.com/)). Since daytime invisibility is the number one homeowner and HOA objection, a launch site that publishes daytime shots, night shots and a warm-white scene for every gallery project will out-prove nearly every incumbent with a trivial content effort.

## Financing and instant estimates are missing from nearly every dealer site

Dealer sites in the category are light on tooling. Click to call, click to text, a Google review widget, a free nighttime demo offer and a quote form dominate, and financing widgets, live chat and visualizers are rare ([Trimlight MN](https://trimlightmn.com/), [Capital Trimlight](https://capitaltrimlight.com/)). The most advanced dealer tool found was Hawkstone Integration's instant estimate, where a homeowner uploads a straight-on photo, sets scale with two clicks, traces the roofline to compute linear feet, then sees recommended products and a "Book My On-Site Estimate" button with a 10 percent online booking discount ([Hawkstone Instant Estimate](https://hawkstoneintegration.com/instant-estimate/)). Gemstone is the only major manufacturer with a customer-facing AI preview, "upload one photo and see how Gemstone Lights could look on your home in about 60 seconds," but it lives on the corporate quote page, not dealer pages ([Gemstone Get a Quote](https://www.gemstonelights.com/get-a-quote/)). Third-party AI mockup tools built for lighting contractors (nvvision, Lighting Mockup Pro) and a holiday installer's multi-photo day-and-night renderer show the technology is available to any dealer ([Lighting Mockup Pro](https://lightingmockuppro.com/), [Santa's Lights Virtual Design](https://santaslightssacramento.com/virtual-design)). No tool found in the category combines an AI render, automatic linear feet, a price range and a financing prequal in one flow, which is exactly the flow roofing already runs (address in, satellite measure, instant price range, contact info to unlock the breakdown) ([QuantaTasks comparison](https://quantatasks.com/blog/roofr-vs-roofle-vs-instantroofer)).

The conversion evidence favors a short list of features. **Wisetack reports prequalified customers are 26 percent more likely to proceed and spend 30 percent more**, and Hearth reports a 17 percent increase in closed estimates from offering financing ([Wisetack Prequal Link](https://www.wisetack.com/features/prequal-link), [Hearth](https://gethearth.com/product-features-financing/)). Podium reports **78 percent of homeowners go with whoever responds first** and 40 percent of service calls arrive after hours ([Podium](https://homeservices.podium.com/features/communications)). A contractor CRO playbook puts click-to-call above the fold at a 30 to 50 percent mobile conversion lift, recommends 3 to 5 form fields, and benchmarks paid-traffic conversion at 2 to 3 percent typical, 5 to 7 percent best in class ([Sheppard CG](https://www.sheppardcg.com/insights/cro-home-services-website-playbook)). On the homeowner side, **80 percent factor online booking into choosing a pro, over 50 percent expect financing, 59 percent expect text updates and 72 percent value fast response and transparent pricing** ([Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-customer-service-report-trends-statistics/)). Business owners underrate this, with only 6 percent naming customer financing as a selection driver in Jobber's owner survey against the 50 percent of homeowners who expect it ([Jobber Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/)). That gap is the opportunity. Vendor numbers are self-reported, and no published A/B data exists for visualizers, video heroes or review widgets in home services, so the tools should be prioritized by the homeowner survey and the response-time data rather than by vendor lift claims.

Pricing transparency is the other whitespace. JellyFish corporate publishes "~$25-35 per foot" and a "$650-850" controller with a dealer caveat, Trimlight has a pricing page, and a Dallas competitor attacks both JellyFish and Gemstone with a flat $25 per foot, lifetime workmanship and 0 percent financing ([JellyFish Pricing](https://www.jellyfishlighting.com/pricing), [Trimlight pricing](https://trimlight.com/about/pricing), [DFW LED vs JellyFish](https://dfwledlite.com/blog/jellyfish-cost-explained)). No dealer site in the sample published a per-foot range or named a lender, though independent installers outside the sample do run Financing pages ([Carolina Accent Financing](https://www.carolinaaccentlighting.com/financing), [Nexwave Financing](https://nexwavelighting.com/financing/)). Stay Off The Roof's quote form dropdown shows minimum project prices ($2,500 permanent trim, $1,500 holiday residential), which is the softest form of transparency that still filters unqualified leads ([Stay Off The Roof](https://stayofftheroof.com/)).

## City pages, speed and schema decide who owns the county

Top-ranking dealers in Phoenix (Stay Off The Roof, Level 5) both have per-city pages or a very long city list, and Stay Off The Roof's Scottsdale page is the model. It runs an "Outdoor Lighting Installation in Scottsdale, AZ" H1, opens by naming DC Ranch, Silverleaf, McCormick Ranch and Old Town, carries five services, one local testimonial, three images, six FAQs, an embedded map with zip codes 85250 to 85266, a paragraph naming 15+ communities and a phone, text and quote form, at 1,800 to 2,000 words ([Stay Off The Roof Scottsdale](https://stayofftheroof.com/scottsdale/)). Corporate metro pages list about 20 municipalities per page (Trimlight Toledo lists 21, Trimlight Dallas 19) and JellyFish Houston links 17 service locations ([Trimlight Toledo](https://trimlight.com/locations/toledo), [JellyFish Houston](https://www.jellyfishlighting.com/houston)). OLP even builds county-level URLs, such as a Seminole County permanent roofline page ([OLP Seminole County](https://www.outdoorlights.com/winter-park/resources/service-areas/seminole-county/permanent-roofline-lighting/)). Search Engine Land's guidance is to prioritize important cities first, stay within two hours' drive, give each page a unique URL, title, H1, project showcase and location-specific reviews, link them from the nav when there are 10 or fewer, and add LocalBusiness schema, citing a notary that ranked first with eight unique city pages ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/guide/service-area-pages)). A single-county launch therefore needs 8 to 15 real city pages, not 100 doorway pages.

Keyword targeting must cover two vocabularies at once, "permanent" and "year-round," plus product and architecture terms and city modifiers. Category SEO guidance lists "permanent Christmas lights installation in [city]," "permanent outdoor lighting near me," "year-round holiday lights install," "RGB soffit lighting installer in [city]" and "app-controlled outdoor lights installation," and Trimlight's own H1 pattern is "Permanent Holiday Lighting in [City]" ([Piggybank SEO](https://piggybankseo.com/permanent-outdoor-lighting-seo-get-more-installs-from-google-and-maps/), [Trimlight Toledo](https://trimlight.com/locations/toledo)). No public source gives monthly volume for the permanent-specific terms, while the generic head terms are large, **"christmas lights" at 368,000 and "christmas lights near me" at 246,000 monthly searches** ([KeySearch](https://www.keysearch.co/top-keywords/christmas-keywords)). The implication is that a permanent installer site should also capture "christmas light installation [city]" intent and convert it with cost and comparison content. Demand peaks September through December with the hardest urgency the week before Thanksgiving, and 20 to 30 percent of revenue comes from January to June bookings, so city and cost pages should be live by June or July to age into the peak ([ChristmasLights.io 2026](https://christmaslights.io/post/marketing-in-2026-how-to-grow-your-christmas-lights-business), [Piggybank SEO](https://piggybankseo.com/permanent-outdoor-lighting-seo-get-more-installs-from-google-and-maps/)). A recommended seven-page SEO core for the category is Permanent Outdoor Lighting, Permanent Christmas Lights, Commercial Permanent Lighting, Pricing and Estimates, Service Areas hub, Gallery and FAQs ([Piggybank SEO](https://piggybankseo.com/permanent-outdoor-lighting-seo-get-more-installs-from-google-and-maps/)).

Technical bars are firm. Core Web Vitals require **LCP under 2.5 seconds and INP under 200 milliseconds**, and mobile conversion drops about 7 percent per additional second of load, with **71 percent of home services visits on mobile** ([PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/core-web-vitals-for-contractor-websites/), [Garrett Handley](https://www.garretthandley.com/contractor-website-conversion-rate-in-2026-why-most-sites-convert-under-3-and-what-to-fix)). A lighting site is image and video heavy, so galleries must lazy-load and autoplay video must stay off the LCP path. The schema map is Organization on the homepage, LocalBusiness with areaServed on city pages, Service on service pages and FAQPage on FAQ pages ([BrightLocal schema templates](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/local-seo-schema-templates/)). The five differentiators of top-converting contractor sites are a sticky call button above the fold on mobile, sub-2-second mobile load, source-attributed call tracking, embedded Google reviews with names and dates, and a 60-second lead response protocol, with **60-second response lifting conversion 391 percent** ([Garrett Handley](https://www.garretthandley.com/contractor-website-conversion-rate-in-2026-why-most-sites-convert-under-3-and-what-to-fix)). Google Business Profile drives 60 percent or more of leads for most home services businesses, so the website's primary job is converting map-pack clicks, which is why the review embed and speed matter more than blog volume ([WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/blog/home-services/home-services-marketing-benchmarks/)).

## Manufacturer dealer pages are lead forms the dealer does not own

No manufacturer gives a dealer a standalone website. Trimlight's 330+ dealers get a location page at trimlight.com/locations/[city] with an embedded quote form, testimonials, a service list and no pricing or financing detail, and "no standalone dealer microsites are evident" ([Trimlight Dealer Locations](https://trimlight.com/dealer-locations), [Trimlight Phoenix](https://trimlight.com/locations/phoenix)). Gemstone's dealer page is the richest format, with a 12-month job count ("411 Jobs Completed in Last 12 Months"), a survey-verified 96 percent satisfaction score, badges (Licensed Electrician, Offers Financing), two before/after images and a quote form, but it lacks service-area definition, pricing, warranty detail, install timeline, financing explanation, team bios, video and promotions ([Gemstone dealer page](https://www.gemstonelights.com/dealers/brilliant-solutions/)). JellyFish routes every inquiry through corporate ("fill out the form to be contacted by the closest location") with no evidence of dealer-branded pages ([JellyFish Find a Dealer](https://jellyfishlighting.com/find-a-dealer)). Oelo requires a listing on oelo.com and delegates leads to the area's highest-tier dealer ([Oelo Dealer Tiers PDF](https://www.oelo.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/OeloDealerTiers2024.pdf)). In every case the manufacturer controls the domain, the SEO and first contact.

What is universally missing from the manufacturer-supplied page is the entire list a partner site should launch with: published pricing, a named financing partner, online booking, an instant estimate or visualizer, team and brand identity, video, HOA support and service-area pages. Even the independent Trimlight dealer domains built on a shared template (trimlightoc.com, trimlightdallas.com) keep pricing "not publicly listed" and name financing only as "specialized finance companies" ([Trimlight Dallas FAQ](https://trimlightdallas.com/faq/), [Trimlight OC](https://trimlightoc.com/)). Flagship-only capabilities that never reach dealer pages, and which a partner program can push down, include Gemstone's AI visualizer and dealer verification badge, JellyFish's smart-home integration pages (Alexa, Google Home, Control4, Nice-Elan), HOA page and DIY page, and warranty registration ([Gemstone Lights](https://www.gemstonelights.com/), [JellyFish Lighting](https://jellyfishlighting.com/)). Dealer marketing support promised on "become a dealer" pages is thin and generic. Trimlight promises a playbook, referred leads and marketing materials for a $5,000 to $10,000 entry with exclusive zip codes, Gemstone requires $2M in revenue and five years in business, JellyFish sells a $20,000 to $25,000 starter kit, and only Oelo quantifies anything, with ad match of $2,500 to $7,000 by tier ([Trimlight Become a Dealer](https://trimlight.com/become-a-dealer), [Gemstone Authorized Dealer Program](https://www.gemstonelights.com/the-authorized-dealer-program/), [Franchise.com JellyFish](https://www.franchise.com/franchise/jellyfish-lighting), [Oelo Dealer Tiers page](https://www.oelo.com/dealer-tiers/)). Nobody promises a dealer website, SEO, paid ads management, a CRM or booking tools.

Warranty is the public battleground. Trimlight claims a lifetime warranty on core components to 50,000 hours, Gemstone offers lifetime on lights, 10 years on parts and one year on labor with 30-day registration, JellyFish is reported at five years product and one year labor, and Oelo at five years ([Trimlight.com](https://trimlight.com/), [Gemstone Warranty](https://www.gemstonelights.com/warranty/), [DFW LED vs JellyFish](https://dfwledlite.com/blog/jellyfish-cost-explained), [Oelo Franchise](https://www.oelo.com/outdoor-lighting-franchise-opportunities/)). Labor coverage and transferability are the fine print competitors attack, so a partner site that states a longer labor warranty on its own Warranty page beats Gemstone's and JellyFish's one year on the page where homeowners compare.

## Jobs run $4,000 to $8,000 at $20 to $40 per foot

Published figures converge. A lighting supply house puts the standard installed rate at **$20 to $30 per linear foot**, a 100-foot home at $2,000 to $3,000, a 150 to 200 foot home at $3,000 to $6,000, a 250 to 400 foot home at $6,000 to $12,000, the average homeowner spend at **$4,000 to $8,000**, and dealer net profit at 30 to 50 percent after materials (30 to 40 percent), labor (20 to 30 percent) and overhead (10 to 15 percent) ([Thunder Lighting Supply](https://thunderlightingsupply.com/how-much-do-permanent-lights-cost/)). Strandr's contractor guide prices basic single-color at $15 to $25 per foot and premium RGB at $25 to $40 per foot, cites a **$7,500 average permanent install versus $1,200 seasonal**, a $3,000 project minimum, maintenance contracts at $200 to $500 per customer and seasonal programming at $300 to $800 per season ([Strandr](https://www.strandr.com/blog/permanent-lighting-year-round-revenue-contractor-guide)). Oelo states an average residential install of $5,000+ and a secondary roundup puts small single-story jobs at $2,500 to $3,500, two-story at $3,500 to $5,000 and large at $5,000 to $8,000+ ([Oelo Franchise](https://www.oelo.com/outdoor-lighting-franchise-opportunities/), [Lawn Lab statistics](https://lawnlabslandscaping.com/blog/permanent-christmas-lights-statistics-2026)). Competitor-reported brand pricing, which should be read as adversarial, puts JellyFish at $30 to $40 per foot and Gemstone at $25 to $42 per foot plus a $650 to $850 controller, with a 150-foot Gemstone job at $4,400 to $7,150 ([DFW LED vs Gemstone](https://dfwledlite.com/vs-gemstone)). A top Gemstone dealer's 411 jobs in 12 months at a $5,000 to $7,500 ticket implies roughly $2M to $3M in annual permanent lighting revenue, which is a derived figure, not a published one ([Gemstone dealer page](https://www.gemstonelights.com/dealers/brilliant-solutions/)).

Conversion and channel benchmarks let the site be valued. Home services websites average **7.8 percent conversion** with premium projects at 3 to 7 percent, a **$144 B2C cost per lead**, $350 to $500 for premium remodels, and a roughly 60-day sales cycle ([WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/blog/home-services/home-services-marketing-benchmarks/)). Median contractor sites convert 2 to 4 percent while the top 6 percent convert 8 to 12 percent on identical traffic ([Garrett Handley](https://www.garretthandley.com/contractor-website-conversion-rate-in-2026-why-most-sites-convert-under-3-and-what-to-fix)). In the Christmas light niche, Google Ads cost per lead runs $50 to $150 at 5:1 to 10:1 ROI, Facebook 3:1 to 8:1 on a $2,000 to $5,000 seasonal budget, and yard signs $5 to $15 per lead ([ChristmasLights.io 2026](https://christmaslights.io/post/marketing-in-2026-how-to-grow-your-christmas-lights-business)). Put together, a county-leading site that draws 200 organic visits a month in season at the 7.8 percent average yields roughly 15 quote requests a month, and at a $144 replacement cost per lead and a $5,000 to $7,500 ticket, each month of peak-season organic performance is worth about $2,000 in avoided ad spend and, at a one-in-four close, roughly $20,000 to $28,000 in booked revenue. Those are derived estimates. No source reported organic lead volume for a permanent lighting site specifically, and the "12 to 16 percent outdoor service conversion" figure that circulates in category marketing has no stated data source ([Inbound Lever 2026](https://inboundlever.com/permanent-holiday-lighting-marketing-2026/)).

## Conclusion

The competitive bar in this category is low in a specific way. Incumbent dealer sites have solved the skeleton and the proof stack but have not solved pricing transparency, financing, booking, instant estimates or dealer-owned local SEO, and the manufacturers have no incentive to solve it for them because the manufacturer's page is designed to own first contact. A partner site that ships the priority-1 list with a per-foot range, a named lender and 8 to 15 real city pages is not matching the best site in the county, it is exceeding what the best dealers in Phoenix, Dallas and Orlando currently publish, and it does so on a domain the contractor owns.

The valuation logic follows from that. The site's worth is not the build cost but the lead engine it replaces, and the published inputs (a $144 home services cost per lead, a 7.8 percent average conversion, a $5,000 to $7,500 ticket and a 60-day cycle) let a program price the launch package against avoided ad spend and booked revenue rather than against agency rates. The two open gaps that would sharpen that math are paid keyword volumes for the permanent-specific terms, which no public source provides, and a real organic lead count from one launched partner site, which would replace every derived estimate in this report with a measured one.
