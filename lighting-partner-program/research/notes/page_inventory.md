## Key Question 2: How do they structure seasonal and color changing content?

### Takeaway
Two patterns exist. Trimlight affiliated sites build one templated page per holiday or event (13 on Trimlight DFW, 7 on trimlight.com) plus a "Used For" or "Functions" taxonomy, while JellyFish, Gemstone and EverLights affiliated sites fold seasons into a single "Holiday" or "Functions" solution page and a visual color showcase on the homepage.

### Cited Findings
- Trimlight DFW has an Events menu with 13 holiday pages (Valentine, Presidents' Day, St. Patrick's, Mother's Day, Memorial Day, Father's Day, Fourth of July, Labor Day, Halloween, Thanksgiving, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas) and a homepage grid "Celebrate Every Moment with Seamless Outdoor Lighting" with a card per holiday linking to each page. — [Trimlight DFW](https://trimlightdfw.com/)
- Trimlight DFW's Halloween page is "heavily templated": headline "Stunning Halloween Lighting By Trimlight DFW", 4.9/5 with 358 reviews badge at top, benefits section, "Why Choose Trimlight", four 5 star testimonials, three spacing/pricing tiers (12", 9", 6" labeled Basic/Premium/Plus), six FAQs, roughly 2,000 words, mirroring the Christmas, Fourth of July and Valentine's pages. — [Trimlight DFW Halloween](https://trimlightdfw.com/events/halloween-lights/)
- Trimlight DFW homepage also has a three card use case block: Holiday Lighting, Gameday Lighting, Security Lighting. — [Trimlight DFW](https://trimlightdfw.com/)
- Trimlight.com corporate nav has a Holiday Lighting submenu with seven product style pages: Christmas, Halloween, July 4th, Easter, St. Patrick's Day, Mardi Gras, New Year's, plus a separate Game Day Lighting page. — [Trimlight Dallas](https://trimlight.com/locations/dallas)
- Get Lit (Orlando) uses an interactive homepage carousel showing the same house under tabs: Daytime, Warm White, Christmas, 4th of July, Halloween, St. Patrick's Day, Valentine's Day. It also has an "RGB Codes" resource page. — [Get Lit](https://getlitdecorativelighting.com/)
- Level 5 Lighting (JellyFish dealer) uses a "Functions" menu: Accent, Security, Game Day, Christmas, Independence Day, Halloween, Valentine's Day, Warm White, separate from its "Solutions" product menu. Its homepage portfolio shows the same home in blue then purple, and a farmhouse in green then multicolor Christmas. — [Level 5 Lighting](https://level5lighting.com/)

- Per holiday pages are an SEO play (one URL per "permanent Halloween lights Dallas" query) rather than a UX need; the templated body proves it.
- The homepage "same house, many colors" tab or carousel (Get Lit, Level 5) is the most common way to make color changing tangible without a separate page.
- "Game Day" is treated as its own use case on JellyFish, Trimlight, Level 5 and Light Up sites, at the same level as Holiday and Security.

### Gaps
- Could not verify whether Trimlight DFW event pages have unique photography per holiday; fetch returned "minimal color scheme details".

### Takeaway
Dealer sites lead with the service and the local crew ("Phoenix's Outdoor Lighting Installers", "we design, you shine") and use the manufacturer brand as a trust badge, while corporate location pages (JellyFish, Trimlight, EverLights) lead with product specs and treat install as one of three pillars.

### Cited Findings
- Stay Off The Roof hero: "Phoenix's Outdoor Lighting Installers", tagline "We design, you shine", "Why Phoenix Chooses Stay Off The Roof" emphasizes "crew quality over product", with Gemstone shown as "authorized Gemstone dealer" badge. Includes "Every Install Includes" list: professional design/installation, property friendly techniques, free scheduled 1 year maintenance, warranty coverage. — [Stay Off The Roof](https://stayofftheroof.com/)
- Outdoor Ambiance: Gemstone Lights authorized dealer badge in hero; proof is "2500+" projects, "20+" years, "2-Year" installation warranty (dealer labor warranty presented separately from product). — [Outdoor Ambiance](https://www.outdoorambiance.net/services/permanent-lighting)
- JellyFish Houston: three pillars "Manufactured by JellyFish", "In-House Technology", "Professional Installation & Warranty", then a fixture gallery (Track, Pathway, Cylinder, Spotlights, Patio) and "five year warranty on parts and one year of labor". — [JellyFish Houston](https://www.jellyfishlighting.com/houston)

## Key Question 5: What proof elements sit on the homepage?

### Takeaway
The standard homepage proof stack is a Google star rating with review count in the hero, a stat row (installs, years, warranty), named testimonials in a carousel, a photo gallery, a warranty or lifetime badge, and a license number for the Arizona dealers; before/after imagery and video are less common than expected.

### Inferences
- True before/after pairs are rare; "same house, different color" pairs and a daytime shot are the industry substitute.
- Review count in the hero (Trimlight DFW 358, Stay Off The Roof 291, JellyFish 1,300+, OLP 4,489) is the single most consistent proof element on the higher ranking sites; the sites without it (Light Up, IAWL, Lighting of Atlanta) were the thinnest.
- License number in the header is an Arizona convention (ROC numbers on Stay Off The Roof and Level 5) driven by state contractor rules.

### Gaps
- Review counts are as reported on the pages at fetch time and were not verified against Google.
- Financing partner names (Wisetack, GreenSky, etc.) were not visible in any fetched homepage; the Financing pages themselves were not opened.
